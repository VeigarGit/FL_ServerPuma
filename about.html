Research

From OPALA to PUMA.

A research effort on adaptive model pruning and efficient federated learning across heterogeneous clients.

The research question

How can different devices
learn together efficiently?

A shared training task does not imply shared resources. Clients differ in data volume, processing capacity, and network conditions. Large updates and slow training can limit their participation.

OPALA studies adaptive structural pruning and personalized aggregation. PUMA extends this direction with explicit client resource awareness, quantization, and aggregation across heterogeneous submodels.

01

Observe the client

Use local data size, training cost, and communication conditions to characterize the client’s budget.

02

Adapt the submodel

Reduce model complexity through structural pruning and compact the transmitted weights through quantization.

03

Aggregate the contributions

Map client submodels to the shared model structure and combine the received updates.

Client Resource-Aware Adaptive Pruning with Heterogeneous Sub-model Aggregation

R. Veiga, J. Sousa, B. Barreto, L. Bastos, D. Rosário, S. Sargento, and E. Cerqueira

OPALA

R. Veiga, R. Morais, R. Bustincio, L. Bastos, D. Rosário, S. Sargento, and E. Cerqueira

A collaboration across institutions

The papers bring together authors affiliated with the Federal University of Pará, the University of Campinas, Instituto de Telecomunicações and the University of Aveiro, and the Federal University of South and Southeast of Pará. See each paper for its author affiliations and funding acknowledgments.

Built on an open research ecosystem

The studies build on PFLlib for personalized federated learning. The implementation uses PyTorch and includes newer research paths for model adapters and vehicular simulation.
